# Measured comparison — 2026-11-04

Every number here is read from the live channels, not estimated. The two tools:

    python3 scripts/report_reach.py    # views, age, views/day per video
    python3 scripts/analyze_reach.py   # the same rows, grouped by hook shape

Both are read-only and use the same `YT_*_REFRESH_TOKEN` env as publishing.
`analyze_reach.py` compares only Shorts **0.5-8 days old**, because a clip in
its first 12 hours reads high (the first push inflates it) and the older ones
have already settled; mixing the two would flatter whatever was posted last.

## 3. Long video vs Short — the watch-hour engine

Watch-hours below are an **upper bound**: they assume the whole video is
watched. Retention is not readable (see the last section), so the true figure is
lower — but the comparison holds, because both sides are bounded the same way.

| | views | duration | watch-hours (upper bound) |
|---|---|---|---|
| night_120 `F4qFHoVpEyI` | 128 | 120.8 min | **258 h** |
| best Short `oIsloIKa0J4` | 1292 | 26 s | **9 h** |

The 120-minute file has **1/10th the views of the best Short and 28× the
watch-hours**. That is the whole reason the long rail exists: the monetisation
threshold is watch-hours, and one long video that holds an audience is worth
more than a hundred Shorts that scroll past. Shorts are the reach engine; the
long videos are the revenue engine.

## 4. K's voice vs the house voice

This is an audio comparison, not a views comparison — the K-tone episode went
live 2026-11-04 04:28Z and has not had time to accumulate a rate.

| | F0-contour r (tones correct) | spectral centroid | MFCC→K |
|---|---|---|---|
| house edge-tts | 1.00 | 1384 Hz | 322.9 |
| OpenVoice direct (old) | 0.33 | 808 Hz | 293.8 |
| **source-filter, alpha 0.55 (shipped)** | **0.90** | 1227 Hz | 315.5 |

K picked 0.55 after listening; it keeps the tones intact (r 0.90 vs the old
0.33) while moving the timbre toward K. The trade-off is real and measured: the
closer to K's own timbre, the duller, because K's recording is duller than
edge-tts (677 vs 1384 Hz).

**Verdict:** ship it. The tone is decided by measurement, the content is
distinct (162 lines, 9 shared stock phrases), and the episode passed verify
(-14.5 LUFS, -3.1 dBFS).

## 5. What this changes

1. The winning-first ordering in `publish_shorts.py` is confirmed: the best
   clip on the channel is the one it put out first.
2. `gen_short_scripts.py` must keep producing only the winning shape per
   channel — a losing hook costs ~2× the views for the same production effort.
3. The long rail deserves the effort: it is where the watch-hours are.

## The number still missing

Retention and impressions need the `yt-analytics.readonly` scope, which the
current refresh tokens do not carry (the Analytics API refuses them). That is
the one number that would say *why* a hook wins; it needs K to re-consent the
channels. Until then, views/day is the honest signal.

## A hypothesis that the data refused

Publishing hour looked like it mattered — the clips posted 18:20-18:21 UTC read
727 and 429 views/day, while a batch posted 06:49 UTC read 103-383. But when the
rows are read in full, the four dead clips (0-3 views in ten days, `oL_LdwFwsb4`
`DHs1j6o0JE0` `qf3O3zbTT_c` `FAEKf4FP_Qs`) are **story-type Shorts**, not the
dharma sleep hooks — a different content type from the very first days of the
channel. The hour and the content type move together, so the hour cannot be
credited. The batch at 06:49 is entirely the losing problem-shape; the evening
posts are tonight-action. **Hour is confounded; do not act on it.** The clean
signal underneath is the one in section 1: dharma sleep hooks pull 100-727/day,
the story Shorts pull ~0. Keep the rail on the sleep material.
